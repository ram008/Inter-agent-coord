## 1. Starting position and target

- Current role: Executive Director at a large enterprise technology company, acting CPO/CTO for a data and AI resilience portfolio of $1B+ ARR and 7,000+ enterprise customers, covering storage-defence and AI-governance products.
- Prior build: a managed-service product grown from about 30 to about 2,600 customers.

- Target: VP Product Management, Senior Director Product Management, or a CPO-track seat. The through-line is trusted enterprise AI — not a generic AI-features role.
- Core lanes, in priority order:

  2. Data and AI security — data security posture, AI security posture, data-loss prevention, and classification/policy enforcement.
  3. Enterprise AI platforms — inference, agents, and governed AI products at scale.
- Standing position, sharpened during October: centralise policy, decentralise enforcement. Governance should index on identity, but identity itself has to change first — from a human credential to an authoritative, short-lived, revocable record per agent.

## 2. How the search ran

- Twice-weekly digest, Tuesdays and Fridays at 08:00 PT: 7 matched roles per digest, each with a fit read and an honest skill gap.
- Review model: about 15 minutes per digest to pick roles of interest; the resume is then tailored and gaps mapped per pick.
- October digests delivered: 9 (2, 6, 9, 13, 16, 20, 23, 27, 30 October), about 63 roles surfaced in the month, plus a separate tracked pipeline and a startup watchlist sweep.
- Rule throughout: nothing is sent, applied on the candidate's behalf, or contacted without the candidate's explicit approval for that specific action. Social posts and anything published under the candidate's name get a preview first and go out only on an explicit yes.
- Resume rules: two pages maximum; fixes to the candidate's own documents are delivered as verbatim find/replace pairs, never as rebuilt copies.

## 9. Learnings — what October taught

1. **Title is not scope.** The strongest fits were not always the highest titles. A Senior Director seat reporting to a CEO / president (Veeam) can be a better GM track than a VP seat with a narrow mandate; conversely, principal-level seats, however well-matched on domain (Optum identity, Upstart agentic platform), are a step down in org leadership and should be filtered on that basis, not on domain alone.
2. **Domain gaps repeat, and they are specific.** The recurring gaps are: deep identity-governance / privileged-access practitioner depth; developer-buyer / CIAM motion; DLP and data-access-governance depth (bridgeable via classification + policy enforcement); and sector overlays (healthcare, energy / critical infrastructure, financial reporting, martech/SMB). Naming the gap and the bridge in one line beats hiding it.
3. **Stale listings are the biggest time tax.** Aggregator listings (Built In, Simplify, job boards) frequently resurface months-old seats. Rule adopted: verify live on the company's own careers page before tailoring a resume for any flagged-stale seat. Freshness signals in the digests (re-crawl date, original publish date) are part of the fit read, not footnotes.
4. **The warm path is the pipeline.** The two applied roles both had a network thread; the digest pipeline produced volume but no applications in the second half of October. The highest-leverage next step is not more digests — it is converting the identified warm paths (Veeam, Zscaler, Okta) with two-step asks, and keeping the Proofpoint path warm through the acquisition.
5. **Acquisition risk is real and fast.** A top-priority role can be deprioritised within three days of an acquisition announcement. Check acquirer news on any target before investing tailoring time.
6. **Remote and location filters bite late.** Several otherwise-strong seats failed only on onsite / hybrid-heavy terms (5 days/week onsite, 50% in-office). Apply the remote filter before the fit read, not after.
7. **Professional-network hygiene matters.** An advisory-entry edit on the candidate's professional profile did not stick during October (the profile still showed older entries instead of the saved set). Lesson: verify after every profile edit, before recruiters land on it — do not assume a save persisted.
8. **Process discipline held.** Two-page resumes, verbatim find/replace document edits, preview-before-publish, and explicit approval per outreach all held through the month. The one process break to fix is the profile-edit verification in (7).
